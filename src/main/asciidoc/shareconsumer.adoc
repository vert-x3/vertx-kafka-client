== Vert.x Kafka Share Consumer

Next to consumer groups, Apache Kafka 4.0 added _share groups_
(link:https://cwiki.apache.org/confluence/display/KAFKA/KIP-932%3A+Queues+for+Kafka[KIP-932]).

With a consumer group, every partition belongs to exactly one consumer. Add more consumers than
there are partitions and the extra ones stay idle. A share group drops that rule: its members read
the same partitions together, each record goes to one of them, and that member acknowledges the
record on its own. In other words a share group behaves like a work queue, and you scale it by
adding consumers instead of partitions.

=== Joining a share group

You create a {@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumer} exactly like a
{@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaConsumer}, from a configuration map, from
{@link io.vertx.kafka.client.common.KafkaClientOptions} or from a `Properties` instance.
The `group.id` is the share group you join.

[source,$lang]
----
{@link examples.KafkaShareConsumerExamples#exampleCreateShareConsumer(io.vertx.core.Vertx)}
----

NOTE: There is no `auto.offset.reset` for share groups. The broker keeps the delivery state of the
group in its internal `__share_group_state` topic and decides where the group starts reading, so a
share consumer never picks a starting offset itself.

Subscribing works as usual, with
{@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumer#subscribe(java.util.Set)} and a record
handler registered through
{@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumer#handler(io.vertx.core.Handler)}.

[source,$lang]
----
{@link examples.KafkaShareConsumerExamples#exampleShareConsumerSubscribe(io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumer)}
----

Since nobody owns a partition, there is no assignment and no rebalance to react to, so you will not
find partition assigned or revoked handlers here. Flow control is unchanged: `pause()`, `resume()`
and `fetch()` all work as they do on a regular consumer.

=== Acknowledging records

Every record a share consumer receives has to be acknowledged, so the broker knows whether it was
handled or should be handed out again. The `share.acknowledgement.mode` property decides who does
that:

`implicit`:: the default. The records from a poll are marked as processed on the next poll or commit.
You acknowledge nothing yourself.
`explicit`:: you acknowledge each record with
{@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumer#acknowledge}. Anything you leave
unacknowledged is released once the acquisition lock expires, and delivered again.

The Vert.x client never sets this property for you. Pick the mode your application needs.

[source,$lang]
----
{@link examples.KafkaShareConsumerExamples#exampleCreateShareConsumerExplicitAcknowledgement(io.vertx.core.Vertx)}
----

In explicit mode, {@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumer#acknowledge} reports the
outcome of one record.

[source,$lang]
----
{@link examples.KafkaShareConsumerExamples#exampleShareConsumerAcknowledge(io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumer)}
----

The {@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.AcknowledgeType} says what happened to it:

{@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.AcknowledgeType#ACCEPT}:: handled, never send it again.
{@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.AcknowledgeType#RELEASE}:: not handled, put it back for anyone
in the group to pick up.
{@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.AcknowledgeType#REJECT}:: it can never be handled, drop it for
the whole group.
{@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.AcknowledgeType#RENEW}:: still working on it, extend the
acquisition lock and return it on the next poll.

[source,$lang]
----
{@link examples.KafkaShareConsumerExamples#exampleShareConsumerAcknowledgeTypes(io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumer)}
----

Releasing a record that always fails would keep it circling the group forever.
{@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumerRecord#deliveryCount()} tells you how many
times the broker has already delivered it, which is what you need to give up after a few attempts.

[source,$lang]
----
{@link examples.KafkaShareConsumerExamples#exampleShareConsumerDeliveryCount(io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumer)}
----

=== Committing acknowledgements

Acknowledging only changes an in-memory state. The broker hears about it when the acknowledgements
are committed, which happens on the next poll, or when you ask for it.

{@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumer#commitSync()} sends what is pending and
completes once the broker has confirmed it.

[source,$lang]
----
{@link examples.KafkaShareConsumerExamples#exampleShareConsumerCommitSync(io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumer)}
----

{@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumer#commitAsync()} sends it without waiting.

[source,$lang]
----

=== Polling explicitly

Instead of registering a record handler you can drive the polling yourself with
{@link io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumer#poll(java.time.Duration)}.

Keep calling it regularly. A member that stops polling for longer than `max.poll.interval.ms` is
removed from the share group.

Start polling as soon as the subscription completes, rather than waiting until you expect records.
The group only begins reading once its members are active, so records produced during an idle gap
right after subscribing may not reach you.

[source,$lang]
----
{@link examples.KafkaShareConsumerExamples#exampleShareConsumerWithPoll(io.vertx.core.Vertx, io.vertx.kafka.client.consumer.KafkaShareConsumer)}
----

committed is released, so it will be delivered again.
